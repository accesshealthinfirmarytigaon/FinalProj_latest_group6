Personal reflections from each group member on the experience of conducting a real-world usability evaluation — the challenges faced, insights gained, and lessons carried forward.

Collective Takeaway

Group Reflection

Group 6 — Overall Experience

Conducting this usability evaluation was one of the most grounding experiences of our HCI course. We often design or build systems with the assumption that users will behave as we expect — but real-world testing quickly dispelled that notion.

Watching respondents interact with our prototype revealed invisible gaps between our intent as designers and the actual experience of end users. Our SUS score of 72.5 is a meaningful number — not just a grade, but a mirror of where our design is strong and where it falls short.

As a team, we leave this project with a deeper appreciation for iterative design, the value of early user feedback, and the importance of designing not just for ourselves, but for the community we serve — in this case, the people of Tigaon.

Key Lessons

What We Take Forward

Test Early

Usability issues discovered late are expensive to fix. Involving real users from the earliest prototype stage would have saved significant rework.

Observe, Don't Guide

The most valuable data came from watching users struggle quietly — not from asking them how they feel. Resisting the urge to help was harder than expected.

Quantify & Qualify

SUS scores gave us numbers; open-ended questions gave us stories. Together, they painted a much fuller picture than either alone could.

Design for the Community

The Tigaon community includes users with varying levels of tech literacy. Designing for the median, not the expert, is the most inclusive choice.
